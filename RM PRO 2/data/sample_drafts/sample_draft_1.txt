TITLE: Decentralized Cryptographic Ledger Architecture for Intellectual Property Provenance and Manuscript Time-Stamping in Academic Research
AUTHOR: Mayur Sanjay Nakate
AFFILIATION: Department of Computer Science, Vishwakarma University, Pune, India

SECTION 1: ABSTRACT
The contemporary peer review and academic publication paradigm faces systemic vulnerabilities, including manuscript scooping, intellectual property disputes, and opaque editorial provenance. This research introduces a decentralized cryptographic ledger architecture that anchors manuscript pre-submission drafts to an immutable blockchain. By leveraging chunk-level Merkle trees and ECDSA digital signatures, researchers establish non-repudiable priority of scientific discoveries without premature public disclosure.

SECTION 2: INTRODUCTION
Scientific priority has historically been governed by submission timestamps recorded within centralized publisher management databases. However, centralized architectures present significant single points of failure, administrative vulnerability, and lack of verifiability by external third parties. Researchers routinely express fear of having proprietary methodologies stolen or scooped during prolonged blind peer review processes. A decentralized proof-of-existence protocol eliminates trusted intermediaries and provides mathematical certainty regarding draft origination.

SECTION 3: CRYPTOGRAPHIC METHODOLOGY
Our proposed protocol employs a dual-layer cryptographic design. The off-chain storage layer partitions manuscript contents into semantic blocks and constructs a balanced binary Merkle tree. The 32-byte Merkle root, combined with an IPFS content multihash and author signature, is embedded into an on-chain transaction. This architecture achieves O(log N) verification complexity and guarantees zero-knowledge selective disclosure: an author can prove existence of a specific equation or claim without exposing unreleased sections.

SECTION 4: EMPIRICAL EVALUATION
Benchmarking across 500 manuscript drafts demonstrates a mean SHA-256 fingerprinting latency of 4.12 ms per megabyte and Merkle root generation of under 12 ms for 1,000 document chunks. Transaction anchoring across simulated consensus nodes verified 100% chain integrity under high throughput. Tamper detection sensitivity reached 100%, detecting single-byte byte modifications instantaneously.

SECTION 5: CONCLUSION
The decentralized manuscript timestamping framework provides researchers with undeniable proof of priority while upholding confidentiality. It establishes a foundation for decentralized science (DeSci) and transparent academic integrity verification.
